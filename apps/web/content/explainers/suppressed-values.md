## Short answer

When a figure would describe so few respondents that a reader could work out one farm's or one household's answer, the publisher withholds it. USDA's National Agricultural Statistics Service marks these cells "(D)", withheld to avoid disclosing data for individual operations. Other programs suppress small counts for the same reason.

The activity still happened and is still included in larger totals, such as the state's.

## What it is not

- It is not a zero. A county with a suppressed harvest grew the crop.
- It is not missing because nobody asked. A value that was never surveyed is a different gap, and this site keeps the two apart.
- It cannot be recovered by subtraction here. Subtracting published counties from a state total leaves the sum of every suppressed county, not any one of them.

## Worked example

If two of three farms growing a crop in a county are large, publishing the county total would let either one estimate the other's harvest. The county's cell is withheld; the state total includes all three.

## Where it is used

The Land and Farms chapter of place pages, the agricultural use cases, and every export, which keep the published status beside each value.
