## Short answer

A 5-year estimate combines sixty months of survey responses into one number. Pooling gives enough responses to publish figures for every county and many small places, and its margins of error are smaller than a single year's would be.

The Census Bureau also publishes 1-year estimates, but only for places with 65,000 or more people. Where both exist, the 1-year figure is more current and the 5-year figure is more precise.

## What it is not

- It is not the value for the last year of the period. A 2020-2024 estimate describes those five years together.
- Overlapping periods should not be compared as though they were consecutive years. 2019-2023 and 2020-2024 share four years of responses, so most of any difference between them is not change.
- A 1-year and a 5-year estimate should not be compared to each other. This site never mixes the two in one card.

## Worked example

To see change over time with 5-year estimates, compare periods that do not overlap, such as 2015-2019 with 2020-2024.

## Where it is used

Most ACS values on place pages and in the use cases are 5-year estimates, labeled with their period.
