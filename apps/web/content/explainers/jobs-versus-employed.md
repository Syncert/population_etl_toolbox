## Short answer

An establishment (payroll) survey asks employers how many people they pay. It counts jobs at the place of work: a person with two jobs is counted twice, and someone who commutes from another county is counted where the employer is. Self-employed people are not in it.

A household survey asks people whether they worked. It counts employed people where they live, once each, including the self-employed.

## What it is not

- The two counts should not be subtracted from each other or added together.
- A county with many jobs and few employed residents is not a contradiction; it may be where people commute to.

## Worked example

A downtown county might have 400,000 payroll jobs and 300,000 employed residents. Both are correct, and the difference is mostly commuting and second jobs.
