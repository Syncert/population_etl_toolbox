## Short answer

A percentile rank says what share of a peer group has a lower value. A county at the 80th percentile for median household income among its state's counties has a higher median than four in five of them.

The rank is only as meaningful as the group. Peers chosen by size, region, or economy give different ranks for the same county, which is why this site always shows how the peers were chosen.

## What it is not

- It is not a measure of distance. The 50th and 60th percentiles can be $500 apart or $15,000 apart.
- It is not a score of how well a place is doing. A high rank on a burden measure is not good news.
- When values carry margins of error, places close in rank may not be distinguishable at all.

## Worked example

Among ten neighboring counties, one with a median income of $78,000 could rank seventh while being within its margin of error of the counties ranked fifth and ninth.

## Where it is used

The peer-benchmarking use case and the peer views in the shared evidence panel.
