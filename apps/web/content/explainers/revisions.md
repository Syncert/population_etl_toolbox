## Short answer

Many statistics are first published before all of their inputs are in. The Bureau of Labor Statistics revises local unemployment figures the following month and again each year when it re-benchmarks them. The Population Estimates Program re-estimates its entire series every year.

This site keeps each release it captured. The latest view shows the current published values; the as-released view shows what was published at the time.

## What it is not

- A revision is not an error being hidden. Publishers announce their revision schedules.
- An old screenshot and today's chart can both be faithful. They describe different releases.

## Worked example

A county's unemployment rate for March might be published at 4.2 percent in April and revised to 4.4 percent the next spring after the annual benchmark. Both are real publications; the second uses more information.

## Where it is used

The explorer's as-released scope, saved charts that pin a release, and the source notes on every page.
