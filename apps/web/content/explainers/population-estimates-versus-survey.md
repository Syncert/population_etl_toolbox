## Short answer

The Census Bureau publishes two population numbers for most places. The Population Estimates Program starts from the last decennial census and adds births, subtracts deaths, and adds net migration, using administrative records, to estimate the population on July 1 of each year. The American Community Survey asks a sample of households over a period and estimates the population from their answers.

The official population for a place between censuses is the Population Estimates Program's. The survey's total is published so that its other tables can be read against the population they describe.

## What it is not

- Neither number is wrong. They answer the question with different methods, dates, and error sources.
- They should not be mixed. A rate built from a survey count divided by a population estimate combines two methods; this site does not compute one.

## Worked example

A county might show 590,000 residents from the Population Estimates Program and 561,000 from the five-year survey. The survey describes an average over five years; the estimate describes one July day.

## Where it is used

The People chapter of every place page shows both, labeled with their programs, and the population-growth use case reads them side by side.
