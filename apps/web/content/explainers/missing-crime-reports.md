## Short answer

The FBI's Uniform Crime Reporting Program collects counts from local, state, tribal, and federal law enforcement agencies, which submit them voluntarily. When an agency does not report for a month, that month has no count from it. It does not mean no crimes happened there.

Participation changed sharply in 2021, when the program moved to the National Incident-Based Reporting System and many agencies had not yet switched. Any total for a place is bounded by which agencies reported.

## What it is not

- It is not a count of crimes that occurred. It counts offenses that were reported to and recorded by agencies that submitted data.
- A gap is not a decline. A year with fewer reporting agencies can show fewer offenses with no change in crime.
- The FBI does not publish these offenses for counties. A county page shows its state's figures, labeled as state context.

## Worked example

If a state's reporting coverage dropped from 95 percent to 60 percent of its population, a lower offense count that year says more about reporting than about safety.
