## Short answer

The Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers tracks the change in prices paid for a fixed basket of goods and services by urban households across the United States. It is an index: it says how much prices rose or fell relative to a base period, not what anything costs.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes indexes for some large metropolitan areas and regions, but no county index. The series on this site is the national one.

## What it is not

- It is not a comparison of price levels between places. An index of 300 in two cities would not mean they cost the same to live in.
- It is not your household's inflation. A household that rents, drives a lot, or spends heavily on health care has its own mix.
- It does not describe any county. Beside a local value, it is national context.

## Worked example

If the index rose from 290 to 300, prices for the national basket rose about 3.4 percent. Rents in a particular county may have risen faster or slower.

## Where it is used

The cost-of-living and housing-affordability use cases show it labeled as national context.
